## Laminar Pour Physics

The traditional *Shui Ping* ("water level") silhouette derives its name from its extraordinary center of gravity—balanced so evenly that a properly finished pot can float level in a basin of water. On the tea tray, that symmetry translates directly into poised pouring dynamics. Its straight, cannon-style spout vents air cleanly while discharging the entire 160-milliliter charge in under seven seconds with crisp, laminar precision. Pressing a fingertip gently over the airhole on the lid knob arrests the stream mid-pour with near-instant responsiveness, allowing ultra-short Gongfu flash infusions without awkward wrist twists or post-pour drip.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

Instead of introducing stainless steel mesh that cools the liquor and interrupts clay-to-leaf contact, this pot integrates a multi-hole domed clay filter directly behind the spout root. When brewing twisted Wuyi rock oolong, Phoenix Dancong, or tightly rolled high-mountain balls at traditional Gongfu leaf-to-water ratios, whole leaves expand into a unified wet mass inside the spherical belly while clear liquor sweeps cleanly through the perforated clay dome. Avoid tiny fannings or broken dust, which can press against the wall openings during aggressive pours.

## Thermal Behavior

Dense *Zhuni* clay shrinks considerably in the kiln, leaving tighter mineral sintering and higher thermal reflectivity than coarser *Zini* or *Duanni* clays. Pre-heated with a brisk exterior rinse of boiling water, our 160-milliliter vessel retained blistering brewing intensity throughout a 30-second steep, coaxing deep stone-fruit esters and silky texture out of high-altitude oolongs without flattening floral brightness. ## Care & Pairing Recommendations

Treat unglazed Yixing Zisha clay with reverence: never let soap, oil, or scented teas near its pores. Immediately after each session, flush thoroughly with hot kettle water, stand the lid beside the pot to vent moisture completely, and wipe external patina gently with a clean linen cloth—practices detailed inside our [Complete Teapot Care Guide](/guides/care-maintenance/complete-teapot-care-guide/).
