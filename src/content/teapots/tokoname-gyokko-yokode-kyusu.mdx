## Laminar Pour Physics

Few vessels in tea culture approach the hydraulic poise of a well-thrown Tokoname *yokode* kyusu, and the Gyokko 320ml earns our highest pour rating. Because the hollow side handle sits perpendicular to the spout, your thumb stabilizes the lid knob while a light rotational flick of the forearm guides the stream. Water exits the tapered spout in a silent, glass-smooth arc and halts the millisecond you level your wrist—leaving not even a clinging bead along the upper rim. Even down to the last precious *shizuku* ("golden drop") of a sencha steep, drainage remains swift and clean.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

Instead of confining leaves inside a narrow hanging metal cylinder, the Gyokko uses a wide, low-slung chamber paired with a built-in fine strainer wrapping the spout wall. Loose needles of steamed Japanese green tea spread freely across the broad floor, bathing evenly in the entire 320-milliliter water column. Because the strainer spans a wide horizontal curve, tiny broken fukamushi particles distribute across hundreds of open perforations rather than clogging a tiny bottleneck, keeping late-pour velocity brisk and clean.

## Thermal Behavior

High-fired Tokoname stoneware balances brisk responsiveness with steady warmth. In solo steeping tests at 70 to 80 degrees Celsius—the sweet spot for Japanese greens—the moderately thick red clay retained roughly 85 percent of starting heat over two minutes while quickly radiating excess scalding intensity away from delicate shoots. That natural buffering protects sweet amino acids without turning leaves grassy or bitter. ## Care & Pairing Recommendations

Unglazed Tokoname clay must never touch dish soap or scented detergents, which sink irreversibly into porous clay walls. Immediately after service, discard spent leaves, rinse under clean warm water, and leave the lid ajar on a breathable tray as recommended in our [Complete Teapot Care Guide](/guides/care-maintenance/complete-teapot-care-guide/).
