## Laminar Pour Physics

The Hario Chacha Kyusu Maru proves that a short, wide-throat glass spout does not have to dribble. During repeated pouring trials in our studio, the stream exits as a cohesive, glassy cylinder up to a moderate tilt angle of roughly 55 degrees. Because the rim of the spout is subtly flared and polished rather than blunt, water detaches cleanly at the lip when you bring the vessel upright. Over-tilting past 75 degrees introduces light turbulence around the rim of the basket, so a controlled, unhurried wrist angle produces the cleanest laminar arc.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

What separates this model from cramped novelty glass pots is its unusually deep 18-8 stainless steel basket. Instead of suspending tea leaves in the top third of the vessel, the mesh cylinder descends within millimeters of the rounded floor. Even a modest 250-milliliter single-cup infusion completely submerges rolled Taiwanese oolongs or long-leaf sencha. When fully hydrated, leaves expand vertically and radially without pressing prematurely against a tight collar, allowing vigorous convection currents to circulate flavor compounds evenly throughout the liquor.

## Thermal Behavior

Clear borosilicate glass prioritizes visual clarity and flavor neutrality over thermal inertia. At 700 milliliters filled with 85-degree Celsius water, our thermometer tracked a drop of approximately 9 degrees Celsius across a four-minute steep at ambient room temperature. While that rapid cooling curve can under-extract stubborn aged pu-erh or heavy Assam black teas unless pre-warmed, it is a genuine advantage for delicate Japanese greens and white teas that turn bitter when overheated. Pre-rinsing the sphere with hot water stabilizes the opening temperature window before you start our [Tea Steeping Timer](/tools/tea-steeping-timer/).
