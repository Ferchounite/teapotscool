## Laminar Pour Physics

Engineered in Japan's historic Mino ceramic district, the Zero Japan Universal earned cafe cult status strictly on pour dependability. Its compact, tapered spout draws from high inside the glazed chamber and terminates in a razor-crisp glazed edge. Across full-speed and slow-trickle pours, the water stream tracks straight into narrow tasting cups with zero sideways splash or dribble down the ceramic chin. Even better, the removable brushed stainless steel lid clips securely into the rim slot, liberating your opposite hand rather than requiring a finger pressed over the lid while tilting.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

Inside the wide top aperture sits a deep, cylindrical stainless steel basket crafted with fine Japanese wire weave. The basket drops nearly to the stoneware floor, allowing a single 200-milliliter afternoon cup or a full 450-milliliter two-cup pot to hydrate leaves completely. Rolled ball oolongs and sprawling Darjeeling leaves receive solid vertical lift, while fine herbal blends stay neatly confined inside the basket walls. Popping the infuser out takes one clean motion, halting extraction before bitterness develops.

## Thermal Behavior

Vitreous Mino stoneware sits in an ideal thermal middle ground between ultra-light glass and massive cast iron. Pre-rinsed with warm water, the 450-milliliter body held water within 5 degrees Celsius of starting temperature over a four-minute infusion—ample thermal endurance for Assam black teas and honey-aroma black blends without scorching greener cultivars. Because the interior carries a smooth, glass-hard food-safe glaze, flavor transfer between sessions is effectively zero. ## Care & Pairing Recommendations

Routine upkeep takes less than a minute: unclip the stainless steel lid, lift out the basket, and rinse the glazed vessel under warm water.
