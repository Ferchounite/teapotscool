## Laminar Pour Physics

Authentic Nambu Tekki foundries in Morioka treat spout casting as precision fluid engineering, and the Iwachu Arare shows why. Its short, upward-sweeping spout ends in a subtly beveled lip that slices cleanly through surface tension at the end of every cup. Across twenty controlled pours from full 650-milliliter capacity down to the final tablespoon, the arc remained cylindrical and predictable with zero running drips down the outer hobnail ridges. The rigid iron bail handle stays cool near the top arch, giving confident balance despite the vessel's dense wall weight.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

Inside the wide top opening sits a deep, cylindrical stainless steel infuser basket with fine laser-punched perforations. Because the rim of the basket rests flush beneath the recessed cast-iron lid, leaves have ample vertical clearance to swell while remaining easily removable the instant steeping finishes. That crisp removal stops astringency cold, whether you are infusing tightly twisted Wuyi rock oolong or broken-leaf Darjeeling. Even fine rooibos fragments stay cleanly trapped inside the mesh rather than drifting into the enamel chamber.

## Thermal Behavior

Heat retention is the defining strength of cast iron. When pre-warmed with a quick splash of hot water prior to brewing, the Iwachu Arare dropped less than 3 degrees Celsius across a five-minute infusion—outperforming thin borosilicate glass by a wide margin. Because the interior is glazed in smooth black porcelain enamel rather than raw iron, it serves strictly as a steeping vessel (*tetsubin kyusu*) rather than a direct-fire kettle. ## Care & Pairing Recommendations

Protecting the enamel rim from moisture and rust is straightforward if you follow consistent habits: never use soap or abrasive scrubbers, rinse immediately with warm water, and wipe the unglazed top lip and lid rim bone-dry while the iron is still gently warm.
