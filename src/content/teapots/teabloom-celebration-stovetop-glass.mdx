## Laminar Pour Physics

Pouring from a 1.2-liter glass sphere can feel unwieldy if the spout sits too low on the belly, yet the Teabloom Celebration maintains solid hydraulic composure. Its gently S-curved gooseneck spout starts below the waterline and tapers toward a crisp, angled cut. During testing at 900 milliliters fill volume, the stream stays smooth and steady without surging until the vessel passes a 65-degree tilt. A subtle drip occasionally clings to the lower lip after the final ounce, so lifting the wrist sharply at the end of each cup prevents stray droplets on table linens.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

This vessel excels in open-chamber brewing. With the glass infuser insert removed, a hand-tied jasmine or amaranth tea blossom has unobstructed horizontal and vertical clearance to bloom evenly across the flat bottom. When the slotted borosilicate infuser sleeve is seated inside, whole-leaf oolongs, silver needle white tea, and large botanical tisanes unfurl freely in abundant water volume. However, finely broken Assam or rooibos needles will slip through the laser-cut glass slits, making whole-leaf selections ideal for clean cup clarity.

## Thermal Behavior

Hand-blown borosilicate glass provides reliable low-expansion durability over a gentle gas flame or electric coil diffuser, provided you keep liquid inside the chamber at all times. Because the 1,200-milliliter footprint exposes a wide top aperture, uncovered water cools noticeably faster than heavy iron or thick stoneware—dropping roughly 8 degrees Celsius over five minutes at full capacity. For unhurried table service, resting the teapot over a tea-light warmer stabilizes extraction temperature, and using our [Tea Steeping Timer](/tools/tea-steeping-timer/) prevents over-steeping delicate floral buds.
