## Laminar Pour Physics

Designed by Masa Fujii for FORLIFE, the 18-ounce (530-milliliter) Stump Teapot is built around cafe workflow efficiency rather than ornate parlor curves. Its short, high-mounted triangular spout starts slightly above the midline and terminates cleanly with a glazed lip that breaks surface tension cleanly during brisk two-cup service. Scoring 9 out of 10 in our pour evaluation, it delivers a steady vertical stream across steep 75-degree tilts with minimal end-of-pour chin dribble. The permanently riveted stainless steel lid opens via an ergonomic rear thumb tab, keeping your opposite hand free throughout the entire pour.

## Strainer & Leaf Expansion Dynamics

Inside the wide cylinder sits a deep, cylindrical extra-fine infuser basket etched with 0.3-millimeter stainless steel perforations. Unlike coarse wire baskets that allow cut English Breakfast fannings or needle-fine South African rooibos to slip through into the cup, these micro-perforations hold back fine botanicals while allowing brisk water exchange through the walls. Because the basket drops deep into the 530-milliliter chamber, rolling oolongs and medium-cut Assam leaves still receive ample room for unfurling in both half-pot (300 ml) and full-pot sessions.

## Thermal Behavior

High-fired, lead-free vitrified ceramic provides balanced thermal mass with neutral taste retention. Pre-warmed with a quick hot-water swirl, the Stump maintains active extraction heat reliably across three- to five-minute infusions for black tea, spiced chai, or toasted barley blends. Its flat, non-porous interior glaze washes clean between heavily spiced blends and delicate white teas without carrying residual oils. ## Care & Pairing Recommendations

Daily cleanup takes seconds: flip open the attached steel lid, lift out the micro-mesh basket to compost spent leaves, and place the entire pot and basket directly in the dishwasher. Never place this vessel on a direct stove burner or inside a microwave—metal hardware in the hinged lid sparks in microwave ovens, and dry burner heat thermal-shocks vitrified stoneware. If tannin staining gradually darkens the pale interior glaze, follow the gentle baking-soda soak outlined in our [Complete Teapot Care Guide](/guides/care-maintenance/complete-teapot-care-guide/).
